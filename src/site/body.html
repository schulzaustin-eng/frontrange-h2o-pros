Treatment options

Match the system to the water—not the other way around.

Whole-home treatment addresses water entering the house. Point-of-use systems may make sense when the primary concern is drinking or cooking water.

01

Whole-home carbon filtration

Intended for taste, odor, and disinfectant reduction when the media, service flow, and certification match the actual water conditions.

02

Water softening

Useful when measured hardness is contributing to scale, spotting, soap inefficiency, or appliance concerns.

03

Reverse osmosis drinking water

Point-of-use treatment for customers who want an additional barrier for drinking and cooking water.

04

Private-well treatment

Requires certified lab results and property-specific hydraulic review rather than a municipal utility average.

Common questions

Straight answers about home water treatment.

Does every Denver home need a softener?
No. Denver’s water is generally softer than water in several nearby communities. Hardness should be measured at the property and evaluated against actual scale or spotting concerns.
Is a whole-home filter the same as a softener?
No. Carbon filtration and softening solve different problems. Some homes may benefit from one system, both systems, or neither.
Can you size a system over the phone?
A call can identify a preliminary equipment category and likely flow target, but final selection requires water testing and installation verification.
What about lead, PFAS, or other health concerns?
Health-related concerns require appropriate testing and equipment certified for the specific reduction claim being discussed.
Do private wells require different treatment?
Yes. Private-well treatment should be based on certified laboratory analysis and the property’s available flow rather than utility averages.
